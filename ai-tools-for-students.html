Best Free AI Tools for Students in 2026

AI tools can help you understand difficult topics, organize notes, practise a language, improve a draft or prepare for an exam. The best choice depends on the task—not on how many apps you install. This guide explains what ten popular tools can help with, how to try them, and what to check before relying on their results.

What does “free” mean?

Many services offer a free tier with limits rather than unlimited access. Features, pricing and regional availability change, so check the official provider page before signing up or planning a project around a specific feature.

10 AI tools worth exploring

1. ChatGPT — explanations and practice

Study assistantBrainstorming

Ask for a difficult concept to be explained at your level, generate revision questions, plan a study session or get feedback on the clarity of your own draft. Give context: your course level, the topic and what you already understand.

Try: “Explain photosynthesis in five simple steps, then ask me five questions. Do not reveal the answers until I try.”

Keep in mind: It can make mistakes. Verify formulas, dates, quotations and important facts using your textbook or teacher’s material.

2. Google Gemini — planning and brainstorming

IdeasRevision

Use Gemini to break a broad subject into smaller questions, compare concepts, suggest examples or plan a presentation. If you use Google services, check which integrations are available to your account.

Try: “I have 45 minutes to revise the causes of the First World War. Create a realistic study plan and finish with a self-test.”

Keep in mind: Treat generated explanations as a starting point and confirm them against reliable course material.

3. NotebookLM — learning from your own sources

NotesSource-based study

NotebookLM is useful when you want to study selected documents, notes or other supported sources. Ask questions about the material, request a summary or build a glossary from the documents you provide.

Try: Add class notes you are allowed to upload, ask for the five main ideas, then ask it to explain unfamiliar terms. Check the answers against the source.

Keep in mind: Only upload material you have permission to share, and review current upload and usage limits.

4. Perplexity — starting research

ResearchSource discovery

Use it to explore a topic and discover potentially useful pages or viewpoints. It can be a convenient first step when you need to find background reading for an assignment.

Try: “Give me an overview of renewable energy storage and link to reliable introductory sources. Separate established facts from open research questions.”

Keep in mind: A citation is not automatically proof. Open each original source and confirm that it supports the claim. Prefer primary research, official statistics and reputable academic sources when appropriate.

5. Khan Academy and Khanmigo — guided learning

Tutoring-style practice

Khan Academy provides lessons and practice, while Khanmigo offers AI-supported learning experiences where available. A guided approach can be helpful when you want hints and questions instead of a finished answer.

Try: Attempt a problem first and request one hint at a time. Explain your reasoning before moving to the next hint.

Keep in mind: AI features, eligibility, availability and pricing may vary. Check current access details.

6. Photomath — understanding maths steps

MathsWorked examples

Photomath can help you inspect steps for many maths problems. Instead of copying the result, compare each step with the method taught in class and ask yourself why each operation works.

Try: After reading an explanation, solve a similar question without the app. If you cannot explain a step in your own words, revisit that concept.

Keep in mind: Some features may require payment. Follow assessment rules and do not use assistance where it is prohibited.

7. Grammarly — proofreading and clarity

GrammarEditing

Grammarly can flag grammar, spelling and clarity issues in supported writing workflows. Use it after you have developed your own ideas and evidence.

Try: Review a paragraph for clarity and ask yourself whether each suggested edit preserves your meaning and voice.

Keep in mind: Review every suggestion and check your institution’s policy on AI-assisted editing.

8. QuillBot — paraphrasing and summaries

Writing support

QuillBot offers writing tools that can help you explore clearer wording or shorten text. Use it to understand a passage or revise your own draft—not to disguise copied work as original writing.

Try: Explain the idea in your own words, compare it with the source, and check that the meaning and technical terms remain accurate.

Keep in mind: Paraphrased ideas still need citations when they come from another source. Review usage limits and academic integrity rules.

9. DeepL — translation and language practice

TranslationLanguages

DeepL can help you understand text in supported languages or compare alternative translations. For practice, translate a short paragraph, review important vocabulary and then write your own sentences using new words.

Keep in mind: Translation can miss context, cultural nuance or specialist terminology. Check important wording with a trusted dictionary, teacher or subject expert.

10. Canva — slides and visual revision aids

PresentationsVisual learning

Canva can help you lay out presentations, posters, diagrams and revision sheets. AI-assisted features may help with early drafts, but the final work should reflect your own understanding and match the assignment brief.

Try: Turn your notes into a one-page revision sheet with a clear heading, five key concepts and one labelled diagram.

Keep in mind: Check generated text for errors and review any template, asset or AI feature restrictions.

How to choose the right tool

  1. Define the task. Decide whether you need an explanation, research leads, proofreading, maths practice, translation or a visual aid.
  2. Start with one tool. Try it on a small task before creating multiple accounts.
  3. Check free limits. Look for message caps, export restrictions, watermarks, account requirements and paid-only features.
  4. Compare fairly. Test two tools using the same example from your actual study needs.
  5. Verify the result. Check important claims against course materials and reliable original sources.
  6. Protect your information. Avoid entering passwords, student IDs, private records or confidential coursework unless you understand the service’s data practices.

A practical 30-minute study workflow

  1. 5 minutes — set a goal: Write down one topic you need to understand.
  2. 10 minutes — learn: Read your course material and use an assistant to clarify only the confusing parts.
  3. 10 minutes — practise: Answer questions without looking at the explanation. Request hints rather than complete answers where possible.
  4. 5 minutes — check: Correct mistakes using your notes and record what you need to revisit.

Use AI responsibly

AI is most helpful when it supports active learning rather than replacing it. Follow your school or university’s rules, cite original sources and disclose AI assistance when required. For research assignments, read the original material yourself. For writing assignments, develop your own argument and use editing tools only where permitted. For maths and science, solve similar problems independently to confirm that you understand the method.

Frequently asked questions

Are these tools completely free?

No. Some have free tiers, while advanced features, higher limits or certain services may require payment. Check official pricing pages because plans can change.

Which tool should a student try first?

Choose the tool that matches your immediate task. A conversational assistant may help explain a topic, NotebookLM may suit source-based revision, and a proofreading tool may help review a draft. There is no single best tool for every student.

Can AI do my assignment for me?

AI can help you plan, learn and review, but submitting generated work as your own may break your institution’s rules. Follow your course policy and make sure you can explain the work you submit.

Are AI answers always accurate?

No. AI systems can misunderstand questions, omit context or make errors. Verify important claims with course materials and trustworthy original sources.
